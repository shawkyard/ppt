## Read this first — assumptions & honesty notes

The request ("website design business… highly likely to buy in Utah") was read as:
**find local Utah businesses that are prime prospects to BUY a website build from you** —
the same kind of client as the Fajita Grill project in this repo. If you actually meant
"find web-design *agencies*" (partners / white-label / acquisition), tell me and I'll redo it.

**Geography:** focused on the Salt Lake valley (Midvale / Murray / Sandy / SLC) so every
prospect is a short drive from — and a believable neighbor of — the Fajita Grill build.
That proximity is the pitch: *"We just built the site for the fajita place on State Street."*

**What "highly likely to buy" means here** (the buying signals I filtered for):
1. **Web-presence gap** — no own domain (only Yelp/Facebook/Instagram), or a thin
   template site → clear, demonstrable pain.
2. **Proven demand** — high review counts / press coverage → they have customers and
   revenue to reinvest, and they visibly *care* about their brand.
3. **A trigger event** — just opened, just rebranded, or opening a new location → the
   exact moment a business shops for a site.
4. **An identifiable decision-maker** you can actually reach.

**Verification honesty:** company names, locations, owners, and web-presence status below
are from public web sources (linked). **LinkedIn:** I only list a profile URL where I could
verify it points at the right person. For everyone else I give a ready-made LinkedIn *search
link* — confirm the exact profile in ~10 seconds before you connect. **Independent-restaurant
owners frequently are not on LinkedIn at all** — for those, the real channel is a phone call
or an Instagram DM, and I've listed those instead. I did **not** invent any names or URLs.

## Repeatable sourcing method (how I found these — do it again next month)

1. **Trigger lists:** Gastronomic SLC, Axios Salt Lake City, SL Tribune "Utah Eats,"
   City Cast Salt Lake, Building Salt Lake — all run monthly "new openings" roundups.
   New + just-rebranded = peak buying intent.
2. **Filter for the web gap:** Google the business. If the top results are Yelp / Facebook /
   Instagram / DoorDash / aggregator pages and **no own domain**, that's a net-new-build
   prospect. If they have a thin template site, that's a redesign prospect.
3. **Get the decision-maker:** press pieces usually name the owner/chef; otherwise call or DM.
4. **Confirm LinkedIn** with the search links above (10-sec check) — but for restaurants,
   assume phone/IG is the real channel.
5. **Higher-ticket verticals convert better than restaurants** — med spas, dental/medical,
   law firms, contractors (roofing/HVAC/remodel), real-estate teams. Owners are on LinkedIn,
   budgets are bigger, and many run terrible template sites. Worth a dedicated pass if you
   want volume beyond the restaurant vertical BBP already has a portfolio piece for.
