# Letter to Kim — Private Placement for the Loyalty Company

Draft prepared 2026-08-13. Fill in the bracketed items before sending.

---

Subject: New venture — need your help on a private placement (call tomorrow?)

Hi Kim,

It's been a while! It's Scott — Alma and I met you back when we signed up with
the syndication attorneys through [mentor program name]. In fact, that program
is where Alma and I met — we're married now, and happily.

I'm writing because we're launching a new company and you were the first
person we thought of for the securities side. Here's the short version:

**The business.** We're starting a loyalty program company — software-based
loyalty programs that we sell, deploy, and support for commercial clients. The
company name isn't final yet. This is my world: I've been one of the top
salespeople in the loyalty industry for a long time, I've trained twenty-five
other reps, and I have a deep pipeline of relationships ready to buy.
Initially we'll deliver on established vendor platforms (we're in talks with
four vendors now), with our own AI-assisted development coming later.

**Where we are.** Events moved our timeline up — we committed to launching
this week, and we already have early investors putting money in. We expect
meaningful cash flow quickly from deals I can close, but we'll need outside
capital to scale properly, and I want to raise it the right way from day one.

**What we need from you:**

1. **A private placement.** We want to structure a raise the way [mentor]'s
   deals were structured — I liked how that worked. (I also did a private
   placement with a company about twenty-five years ago, so the concept isn't
   new to me, but the rules may as well be.) I know I can't make claims or
   talk securities with investors until we do this properly, and I want to
   make sure we're inside the lines from the start. At least one likely
   investor is a close friend of twenty years who meets the accredited
   investor criteria.

2. **Equity compensation for the team.** I need to recruit top industry
   talent I can't pay full price in cash. I want to offer fair packages of
   below-market cash plus stock/options. We'd start with roughly six to eight
   people and grow to about ten. I need to understand how to set that up
   legally alongside the raise.

3. **The basics.** What structure you'd recommend for a software/services
   company like this, what the process and timeline look like, and
   approximate costs — I remember your ballpark from the [mentor] deal days,
   but I'd like to know how it translates to a small software company.

**The ask:** can we get a call on the calendar tomorrow, or as soon as you
have an opening? I'd love thirty minutes to walk you through it and find out
exactly what you need from us to get this rolling.

Looking forward to catching up — Alma says hello!

Best,
Scott [last name]

## Prep notes for the call (not part of the letter)

- Likely topics Kim will raise: Reg D 506(b) vs 506(c) (general solicitation
  vs. existing relationships), accredited-investor verification, whether the
  $100K/15% handshake deal needs to be papered inside the offering, and an
  equity incentive plan (option pool) set up alongside the entity structure.
- Have ready: the investor cost brief, the two handshake investor
  conversations ($100K/15% and the next $100K at ~3%), and the list of the
  four vendors.
- Flag to Kim: money has already been discussed/accepted from early
  investors — ask how to paper what's already in motion correctly.
