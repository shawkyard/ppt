# Deep Dive — Obsolete Electrical Trip Units & Breaker Frames

**Prepared:** 2026-07-07 · Companion to `SCARCITY-SOURCING-REPORT.md` (Rank 3/Profile C & Rank 9/Profile I) · **Purpose:** operationalize the lowest-fraud, most-repeatable broker niche into a first deal.

> **Why start here.** Unlike memory/SSD/optics, this market is **obsolescence-scarce, not demand-shock scarce.** Prices don't swing daily, counterfeiting is minimal (little incentive to fake a used trip unit), the parts are small and rugged, and there is an established dealer network you can broker *between*. The catch is a different risk — **quality/liability of used protective gear** — which the industry already solved with a certification standard (PEARL). Work inside that standard and this becomes a clean, repeatable $300–$3,000-per-deal business.

---

## 1. The market splits in two — pick your lane deliberately

| | **Lane A — Industrial / Commercial power** *(recommended)* | **Lane B — Residential legacy** |
|---|---|---|
| Typical parts | LVPCBs, MCCB frames, **electronic trip units**, **rating plugs**, retrofit kits | FPE Stab-lok, Zinsco, Pushmatic, Bulldog, Challenger breakers |
| Buyers | Facility/plant electricians, data-center FM, hospitals, campuses, industrial MRO, switchgear shops | Homeowners, landlords, property managers, home inspectors' referrals |
| Value/unit (EST) | **$300–$5,000+** | **$30–$300** |
| Urgency driver | Critical feeder down; full switchgear swap = downtime + permits | Panel is obsolete/unsafe; can't add/replace a breaker any other way |
| Fraud risk | Low (quality risk managed via PEARL) | Low-moderate (relabeled amperage exists) |
| Deal size fit ($500–$5k) | **Yes, per unit or small lot** | Only in bulk lots |
| **Ethical flag** | Standard reconditioning | **FPE & Zinsco are known to fail to trip** — safety-recommended fix is full panel replacement, not a used breaker. Broker these only as like-for-like keep-it-running parts, and say so honestly. |

**Verdict:** run **Lane A** for the $500–$5k target. Lane B is real, steady volume but the money is in truckload lots, and the residential safety brands carry a reputational/ethical footnote you shouldn't paper over. Everything below focuses on Lane A.

## 2. Target part families (Lane A) — where the scarcity + margin actually is

The highest-value-density, most-repeatable items are **electronic trip units and rating plugs** — they're small (cheap to ship/insure), model-specific (real scarcity), and expensive to source new (or simply unavailable).

### 2.1 GE / ABB — Spectra RMS & AKR
- **Frames:** `SE150`, `SF250`, `SG600`, `SK1200` (Spectra RMS MCCB); `AK`/`AKR` low-voltage power breakers
- **Trip units:** MicroVersaTrip, MicroVersaTrip Plus/PM, Spectra RMS electronic trip
- **Rating plugs:** `SRPK`, `SRPG`, `SRPE`, `SRPF` series (e.g. `SRPK800A800`, `SRPG400B150`) — new list ~$106 but frequently on lead/obsolete; used demand steady
- **Signal:** older `TF`/`TK` MCCBs obsoleted → Spectra transition; MicroVersaTrip units aging out of support
- **Broker note:** rating plugs are the ideal *first* item — tiny, high value density, model-locked, low fraud

### 2.2 Westinghouse / Cutler-Hammer / Eaton
- **Frames:** `DS`, `DS-II`, `DB`, `DA`, `Magnum` (low-voltage power); Series C molded case
- **Trip units:** **Digitrip RMS** (510/810/1150 etc.) — heavily retrofitted, high demand
- **Signal:** Eaton acquired Cutler-Hammer; many lines discontinued but installed base is enormous → durable replacement demand
- **Retrofit crossover:** **Digitrip retrofit kits** exist (buyers may prefer these — see §4)

### 2.3 Square D / Schneider
- **Frames:** `Masterpact` (NW/NT and legacy), `PowerPacT` P- & R-frame, older `PB`/`DS`
- **Trip units:** **Micrologic** — `S164A` (6.0P LSIG), `S144A`, `S163A`, Micrologic 5.0/6.0 variants (confirmed catalog PNs)
- **Signal:** obsolescence of specific Micrologic/PowerPacT SKUs with no drop-in modern equivalent short of a breaker swap

### 2.4 Rating plugs, sensors, accessories (cross-brand)
- Rating plugs, secondary disconnects, MOC/TOC switches, charging motors, close/trip coils, auxiliary switches
- **Why:** a whole reconditioned breaker can be blocked for want of one $200 accessory — high urgency, tiny shipping cost, excellent value density

## 3. The quality gate that makes this legitimate — PEARL

Used protective gear that fails to trip is a **life-safety and liability** problem. The industry's answer is **PEARL — the Professional Electrical Apparatus Recycling League** — which sets reconditioning standards (aligned with ANSI/NEMA and ANSI C37.59 for reconditioned breakers) and certifies technicians/members.

**How you use it as a broker:**
- **Source from PEARL members** where possible (Circuit Breaker Sales is a charter member; American West Surplus has PEARL-certified techs). Buying from a PEARL shop de-risks the quality question.
- **Sell the certification.** "Reconditioned and tested to PEARL/ANSI C37.59 with a test report" is the phrase that closes cautious facility buyers and justifies your margin.
- **Require a test report + warranty** on anything reconditioned. New-surplus (never installed) is cleanest; reconditioned is fine *with documentation*; "untested pull" is where quality risk lives — price and disclose accordingly.

This is the equivalent of the SPD/SMART/datecode verification in the other categories — **it's the artifact that lets you broker before you touch inventory.**

## 4. Know your competition — the retrofit alternative

Before you quote a used trip unit, know the buyer may have a **better option than a used part**: **retrofit kits** that drop a *modern* electronic trip unit into their existing breaker while **maintaining the UL listing**.

- **Utility Relay Company — AC-PRO / AC-PRO-II** (very common)
- **Eaton Digitrip retrofit kits** (for Westinghouse/CH frames)
- **ETC-12 / ETC digital trip kits** (480V/600V power breakers)

**Why this matters:** if a buyer's real need is *reliable protection*, a new retrofit kit may beat your used trip unit on warranty and longevity. **Turn this into an outreach advantage, not a threat:** offer both. "I can get you a tested used `S164A`, or if you'd rather modernize, an AC-PRO retrofit — which fits your maintenance plan?" You look like an expert, not a parts flipper, and you can broker either.

## 7. First-deal plan (concrete, 24–48h)

1. **Pick one high-value-density item to master first:** a **GE `SRPK`/`SRPG` rating plug** or a **Square D `S164A` trip unit**. Tiny, model-locked, cheap to ship, real scarcity.
2. **Find demand first:** scan Surplus Record listings, Mike Holt / ElectricianTalk threads, and LinkedIn MRO posts for anyone needing that family. Aim for **≥2 live buyer signals** before sourcing.
3. **Price the supply:** RFQ 3–4 dealers from §5 with the supplier template. Log price, condition, PEARL/test docs, warranty, lead time.
4. **Compute the real spread:** best *documented* supplier price vs. buyer's pain point, minus insured shipping and any escrow fee. Clear **$300–$1,000+**? Proceed. (Rating plugs/trip units routinely support this.)
5. **Structure capital-light:** buyer PO/deposit before you pay, or drop-ship dealer→buyer. Use escrow/inspection on a first deal with a new dealer.
6. **Close on documentation:** deliver with test report + warranty statement + nameplate/serial photos; ship insured.
7. **Bank the relationships:** one reliable PEARL dealer + one repeat facility/MRO buyer = a repeatable pipeline. Log everything.

---

## 8. Standing rules for this niche

- **Never broker protective gear without a condition statement.** New-surplus > reconditioned-with-test-report > untested pull. Price and *disclose* accordingly — a breaker that fails to trip is a life-safety event.
- **Lead with PEARL / ANSI C37.59.** It's your authentication artifact and your margin justification.
- **Offer the retrofit option.** It makes you the expert and lets you win the deal either way.
- **Confirm frame compatibility** (frame + trip unit + rating plug must match) before quoting — the #1 non-fraud way to blow a deal here.
- **Residential FPE/Zinsco/Pushmatic:** if you touch Lane B at all, be honest that the safety-recommended fix is panel replacement; broker used breakers only as explicit like-for-like keep-running parts.
- **Compliance/shipping:** low risk, but confirm end-use and destination for any export; ship insured.
