01

Understand Alma in 30 seconds

A brand does not know whether loyalty will make money. A vendor does not know which brands can afford it. Both guess, both waste money, and bad-fit deals churn. Alma runs the math for both sides and proves the fit with evidence — so the right brand meets the right vendor at the right time.

For a 10th grader · Part 1

Alma is like a matchmaker with a calculator. Loyalty programs (points, rewards, VIP) only make money for some companies. Alma figures out which companies those are, which software company should sell to them, and whether it will actually pay off — then it keeps watching everything so it always knows what changed today.

What it decides
  • Which brands can financially support loyalty
  • Which vendors fit those brands
  • Which accounts show buying signals now
  • Which technology & programs changed
  • What the salesperson should do today
  • How to make a live program better
  • Carrying the model into renewal
Part 3 · The brand promise

"Everyone wins when the fit is right" is the correct promise — keep it.

It is the rare positioning that is simultaneously the ethic (don't sell bad fits), the product (a fit engine), and the growth loop (both sides invite the other). The only risk: "everyone wins" can sound soft. Pair it always with the hard line below so it reads as disciplined, not utopian.

Say this to vendors

Stop burning six months chasing companies that were never going to make money with loyalty.

Say this to brands

Know whether loyalty can make money before you spend millions building it.

02

The honest product read

You asked me to act as CPO, CTO, Head of UX, VP Loyalty, VP Revenue and investor — and to challenge the concept, not applaud it. Here is where I'd push.

What is genuinely strong

The wedge is real and specific. "Salespeople fear the ROI math" is a founder insight you cannot get from a prompt. Deriving members down from Revenue ÷ AOV ÷ Frequency — instead of asking "how many members do you expect?" — is the whole product in one move. Protect it.

What to combine

Collapse "Company Intelligence Record" and "Financial Model" into one object with tabs — the model is a lens on the company, not a separate screen. Collapse Quick / Detailed / Upload calculators into one calculator with a depth slider. Five scores stay five; do not merge them.

What must stay separate

The five scores (never one mystery AI number). Actual vs Estimated provenance (never blended silently). Paid placement vs independent ranking (never let money move rank). These three separations are the trust product.

Part 22 · Do NOT build yet

Voice AI outreach, coalition modeling, 500k-company crawl, the adjacent-market calculators (CDP/ESP/POS ROI), vendor self-serve portal, and CRM two-way sync. All real — all after you've proven the core loop on ~150 vendors and a few thousand hand-curated brands. Breadth is the reward for depth, not a substitute.

The one thing that will kill this if you get it wrong

False precision. The moment a salesperson forwards a report that says "$8,384,227" and the brand's CFO finds it's an AI guess, the trust product is dead. Every number must wear its provenance and its confidence. This is a design constraint, not a footnote — it drives the entire visual system below.

The classic spreadsheet mistake is counting only the interaction term. Alma sums all three effects — and never calls the Benefit-Cost Ratio "ROI×".

Show the math → how Conservative / Expected / High are built
Each driver (enrollment, active rate, AOV lift, PF lift, breakage, incrementality) has a range from the benchmark library, tightened by any actuals. Conservative pulls each driver toward its pessimistic bound; High toward optimistic. We show the band, not a point. The headline is almost always: "attractive even at the low end" or "only works if X — validate X first."
  • Gross incremental = (AOV-only) + (PF-only) + (interaction), across active members.
  • × incrementality × gross margin = economic benefit.
  • − rewards, software, implementation, services, marketing, ops, tech, internal labor = net contribution.
  • Outputs: BCR, Net ROI, Payback, Break-even lift, 5-year outlook.

Expansion, the data moat & "why not just use ChatGPT?"

Parts 17 · 18 · 19 · 48 · 49

Loyalty stays the center. But the same brands buy the technologies around loyalty — so one deep Brand Intelligence Record is reusable across CDP, ESP, POS, personalization fit and ROI. Build the brand once; monetize it many times.

Part 17 · follow the integrations outward

Map who the major loyalty platforms (Yotpo, Antavo, Talon.One, Snipp, Capillary, LoyaltyLion, Salesforce Loyalty…) integrate with. Those adjacencies define the expansion order: Commerce → CRM → CDP → ESP → POS → Personalization → Automation → Payments → Service → Analytics. Same buyers, more surface.

Part 19 · the answer to "I'll just ask an LLM"

The model is not the product. Anyone can rent an LLM; nobody else has the accumulated system: proprietary formulas, field-level provenance, benchmark libraries, vendor capability taxonomy, tech-stack history, buyer-intent history, outcome data and the feedback loops. Anyone can buy a hammer — that doesn't make them an architect.

Highest-value proprietary datasets to hoard first (Part 49): outcome data (screened vs unscreened win/churn), the vendor capability & integration graph, tech-migration histories, and calculator-derived first-party intent. These take years to recreate and get more valuable with every deal.

Part 48 · scaling 30k → 500k economically

Never uniform deep-crawl. The research ladder + caching means 95% of companies live cheaply at L0–L2; you spend L3–L5 only on accounts a paying customer is actually looking at. Cost scales with attention, not with database size.

18

Risks, trust & what needs a human

Parts 29-34
Part 29 · product risk

The system feels magical but the data is thin at first. Mitigate with brutal honesty: show "Missing" proudly, curate the demo universe, and never let breadth outrun trust.

Part 30 · data risk

Stale, mis-attributed, or over-confident data. Mitigate: timestamps, confidence, corroboration rules, and "do-not-research-before" freshness gates.

Part 31 · AI risk

Hallucinated migrations, invented certainty, fabricated rankings. Hard rule: LLMs draft and correlate; they never mint an "Actual" or a rank without evidence.

Part 32 · legal / trust

Rankings must be defensible; competitive notifications must respect permissions; scraped data must respect ToS & privacy. Digital-demand labeled, never sold as audited revenue.

Part 33 · needs human review

Any number that leaves the building in a proposal, any competitive ranking a rep will act on, any "Actual" promotion, and any renewal value claim.

Part 34 · avoiding false certainty

Show bands not points. Lead with the conservative case. Wear provenance and confidence on every figure. Say "not enough verifiable information" out loud when it's true.

Part 50 — How we beat Hostinger Horizons

Horizon will ship a good-looking app. It cannot ship judgment. The bake-off isn't won on components rendered — it's won on whether a loyalty executive believes the thing is telling the truth. That's our entire game.

  1. Win on trust architecture, not screen count.

    Provenance, confidence, and the conservative-case-still-wins framing are things a generic app-builder won't think to build. It's the hardest thing to fake and the easiest thing for a domain expert to spot missing.

  2. Derive members down from business reality.

    Revenue ÷ AOV ÷ Frequency → addressable → enrolled → active. Horizon will ask "how many members do you expect?" We answer the question the customer can't.

  3. One protected engine, five separate scores.

    No black-box "AI score." Every number drillable to its source. That reads as rigor; a single mystery number reads as a toy.

  4. Make it feel alive.

    "What changed overnight," visible agents, Top-3 movement, the research ladder. Daily addiction for vendors, weekly value for brands — designed retention, not a dashboard.

  5. Show the compounding moat.

    Formulas + data + history + vendor graph + outcomes. Explain, on screen, why "just use ChatGPT" misses the point. An investor needs to see the moat, not infer it.

  6. Organize the complexity, don't amputate it.

    Rookie understands it in 30 seconds; a CTO drills for three hours. Progressive disclosure is the differentiator — the ambition stays, the confusion goes.

Everyone wins when the fit is right.
