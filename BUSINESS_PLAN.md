# AI Services Business Plan

**Local AI Services for Blue-Collar Businesses**

*Model: cold-outreach-only, 60 minutes/day, AI agents do the heavy lifting.*

> Wherever you see `[YOUR CITY]`, swap in your metro area. The reference case in this plan is Charlotte, NC + tree services, but the model works in any mid-size or larger metro with any high-ticket, low-tech local trade.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This business sells a simple, high-value digital bundle to local blue-collar service companies that have a weak or nonexistent web presence:

1. **A fully built and hosted website**
2. **An AI lead-intake agent** (a speed-to-quote form that gives customers an instant estimate and captures their info for follow-up)
3. **A Google Business Profile cleanup**

**Price: $500 flat**, with a **$100/month maintenance upsell** (content refreshes, review updates, keeping the site fresh).

The entire operation is run by a fleet of five AI agents. The human operator's job is limited to ~60 minutes per day of the only work that directly makes money: **outreach** — texts, calls, and in-person visits. Everything else (lead research, script writing, website production, fulfillment, ongoing account maintenance) is delegated to agents.

**Primary goal:** land the first paying client (Stripe invoice paid, money in the account — not a verbal yes) purely through cold outreach, then repeat the system.

### Why this works

- The offer is priced as a no-brainer. If it fails, it won't be because of price.
- The lead magnet strategy (build the website *first*, then give it away free) makes the first pitch nearly risk-free for the prospect.
- The target market is enormous, underserved, and easy to find: real scraping runs surface hundreds of local businesses per metro, of which a large fraction have broken, hijacked, or nonexistent websites.
- One additional job per month is worth thousands of dollars to these businesses, so a $500 one-time cost pays for itself with a single lead.

## 2. Target Market

### Primary niche: Tree services in `[YOUR CITY]`

Why tree services are the ideal beachhead:

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| High ticket | Average job is multiple thousands of dollars — one extra client/month justifies the price many times over |
| Fragmented | Not yet consolidated by private equity; mostly mom-and-pop operators |
| Emergency-driven | After-hours and emergency inquiries are common; the first company to respond usually wins the job |
| Low tech adoption | Most don't have a working website; many "websites" are 404s, expired domains, or hijacked redirects |
| Easy to find | Fully discoverable via Google Maps / Google Search scraping |

### Backup niches (same profile, run the same playbook)

1. Commercial pressure washing
2. Junk removal
3. Concrete / driveway installation

All are blue-collar, high-ticket, traditionally non-tech-savvy trades where one additional client per month means thousands in added revenue.

### Market evidence (from the reference run in Charlotte)

A single agent-run scrape found **494 raw businesses**, qualified down to **98 active, verified leads**. Of the first five leads inspected:

- 2 had domains that returned blank pages or 404s
- 1 had an unreachable site
- 1 had a domain silently redirecting to a crypto gambling site
- 1 had an SSL error scaring visitors away ("attackers might be trying to steal your information")

The remaining ~90 leads either had no website at all, only a Facebook/Nextdoor page, or a site with no fast way to request a quote. This is the opportunity.

## 3. The Offer

### Core bundle — $500 one-time

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| Website | Modern, mobile-first, one-page-plus site, built and hosted for them |
| AI lead intake | Speed-to-quote form embedded on the site: instant estimate for the visitor, captured contact info for the owner, works 24/7 including after hours |
| Google Business Profile cleanup | Fix categories, hours, photos, description, and review presentation |

### Upsell — $100/month account management

Ongoing maintenance: fresh content, review updates, and site upkeep, handled entirely by the Account Manager agent. This converts one-time projects into recurring revenue.

### Pricing philosophy

$500 is deliberately underpriced. The goal at this stage is speed to first clients and proof of the system, not margin maximization. The price can (and should) rise once there are case studies and referrals. The offer must be so obviously good that price is never the objection.

### The lead magnet twist

We do **not** pitch the bundle cold. The website is built *before* the first conversation and given away free as the hook:

> "I see you don't have a website. I built you one. Here's the link — it's yours, no catch. If you want me to install and host it for you, let me know."

This is truthful (the preview genuinely is free), disarming, and creates reciprocity. The $500 covers installation, hosting, the AI intake agent, and the GBP cleanup — which nearly every interested prospect will want.

## 4. Operations: The Five-Agent Fleet

The entire business runs on five AI agents. The human does outreach; the agents do everything else.

### Agent 1 — The Prospector (lead research)

- Scrapes Google Maps + Google Search for the target trade across the metro and all surrounding towns ("tree service", "tree removal", "tree trimming" per city)
- Targets 300+ raw results before deduplication
- Captures per business: name, phone, phone type (mobile vs. landline), website, Google rating, review count, city
- Cleans the list: dedupes, filters to mobile numbers only, verifies each business is active and actually serves the metro, classifies website status (none / broken / valid)
- Delivers a master lead table with a summary: total found, total with mobile numbers, total with **no website (priority prospects — flagged)**
- Also builds the **live pipeline dashboard** (see §7)

### Agent 2 — The Opener (outreach copy)

Writes every script: first-touch texts, cold-call scripts, in-person door scripts, reply/objection handling, and opt-out confirmations.

**Non-negotiable rules for first-touch texts:**

- ≤160 characters
- No links, no emojis
- No mention of AI, websites, marketing, or any offer
- Sound like a local human, never a company; sign off with just a first name
- At least one personalization variable (city, first name, company name)
- The only goal of text #1 is **a reply**, not a sale

### Agent 3 — The SiteSmith (website production)

- Churns out high-quality one-page concept sites for priority leads *before* outreach
- Sites are the lead magnet: sent free once a prospect replies with interest
- Volume production on autopilot so outreach is never bottlenecked by fulfillment

### Agent 4 — The Fulfiller (paid delivery)

- Takes over the moment an invoice is paid
- Puts the site live with the hosting provider, installs the AI intake form, performs the GBP cleanup
- Runs the engagement end-to-end so the operator only maintains the relationship

### Agent 5 — The Account Manager (recurring upsell)

- Delivers the $100/month maintenance plan: content refreshes, review updates, keeping the site current
- Fully automated monthly recurring work = near-pure-margin recurring revenue

## 5. Go-To-Market: The Outreach Playbook

### Constraints (by design)

- **100% cold outreach.** No audience, no network, no ads.
- **≤60 minutes/day.** That's roughly **50 outreach touches/day** (texts + calls + free-site deliveries + follow-ups).
- Only do work that moves the needle. Outreach is the only work that makes money; everything else is delegated to agents.

### The sequence

1. **First-touch text** (from the Opener's script pack), e.g.:
   - A: "Hey, do y'all still do tree removal in `[CITY]`? Found you on Google — `[NAME]`"
   - B: "Hey, is this the owner of `[COMPANY]`? Are y'all still taking tree removal jobs around `[CITY]`? — `[NAME]`"
   - C: "Hey, `[NAME]` here — found your company on Google looking at tree services around `[CITY]`. Quick business question: do y'all still handle tree removal there?"

   Casual, local, human ("y'all"), no pitch. Default to option A — short and sweet.

2. **Reply pitch** (once they respond yes):
   > "Awesome — I actually reached out for a different reason. I build tree companies a new lead-converting website and put an AI on the site that answers and books inbound leads 24/7. I already built yours so you can see it — it's completely free. Want me to send it over?"

   **Has-a-website variant:** same structure, but the angle is their current site loses after-hours leads and has no fast quote path — "I can build you a free mobile site that gets leads to a quote request faster. Want me to send it over?"

3. **Objection handling** (scripted):
   - *"What's the catch?"* → It's a free one-page preview, zero obligation. Anything beyond that gets quoted up front; nothing happens without your OK.
   - *"Who is this?"* → "I'm `[NAME]`, here in `[CITY]`. Found you on Google, couldn't find a website for your company, and I build simple sites for local tree companies."
   - *"How much?"* → The one-pager I sent is free, period. Anything beyond it gets a price up front — never an automatic signup.
   - *"Not interested."* → "No problem, thanks for letting me know. I won't follow up or keep pitching you."
   - *"Is this a scam?"* → "Not at all — the free site is me providing value before pitching anything. Happy to hop on a call or swing by your office."
   - *Opt-out* → "Understood — I've removed your number and won't text you again." (And actually do it.)

4. **Escalate hot leads to in-person.** Face-to-face with the owner is the highest-converting close. Offer to swing by their office.

5. **Close and invoice.** Send the Stripe invoice for $500. The deal counts only when the invoice is **paid**.

6. **Pitch the upsell at handoff.** "Everything's live. If you want us to keep it fresh — content, reviews, maintenance — that's $100/month."

### List exhaustion rule

If the first niche list is exhausted without a booked sales call, re-run the exact same Prospector workflow against the backup niches (pressure washing, junk removal, concrete/driveways).

## 6. Financial Plan

### Startup costs

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Agent platform credits | ~$150 per full metro lead-scrape run at max reasoning (cheaper at lower reasoning effort; the reference run cost $151 for 494 raw → 98 qualified leads) |
| Hosting (per client site) | ~$5–20/mo, billed into the offer |
| Stripe | Pay-as-you-go (2.9% + 30¢) |
| Phone/texting | Existing phone or a low-cost business line |
| Domain (own brand, optional at start) | ~$12/yr |

Total to first client: **under ~$200–400** in real spend. Reasoning-effort selection is the main cost lever on agent runs — use max only where the task genuinely needs it.

### Unit economics (per client)

| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue (bundle) | $500 |
| Direct cost (hosting yr 1, agent runtime, Stripe fees) | ~$50–80 |
| **Gross margin** | **~85%** |
| Upsell (if taken) | +$100/mo at near-pure margin (agent-fulfilled) |

### Illustrative ramp

Assume 50 touches/day, 5 days/week ≈ 1,000 touches/month. Even at conservative rates (5% reply → 30% interested → 30% close), that's ~4–5 paid clients/month:

| Month | New clients | One-time revenue | Recurring (50% take upsell) | Cumulative MRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | $1,500 | $150 | $150 |
| 2 | 4 | $2,000 | $200 | $350 |
| 3 | 5 | $2,500 | $250 | $600 |
| 6 | 6/mo | $3,000/mo | $300/mo | ~$1,500 |

Levers after proof: raise the price (the bundle supports $1,500–2,500 easily), add niches, add metros, referrals from happy clients.

### The scoreboard

The single metric that matters at launch: **days from first outreach to first paid invoice**. Verbal yeses don't count; the counter stops when money hits the account.

## 7. Tracking: The Pipeline Dashboard

Built by the Prospector agent as a live web artifact. One bold, readable page tracking the full funnel:

- Leads found
- Texts sent
- Replies
- Interested replies
- Free sites delivered
- Calls booked
- $ closed
- Recent activity feed (date, business, stage change)

Update it as the source of truth every day. If a stage's conversion rate stalls, fix that stage's script or asset — not everything at once.

## 8. Roadmap & Milestones

**Phase 1 — Build the outreach machine (Day 1, ~30 min of setup)**
- Build the Prospector agent; run the metro scrape (300+ raw → cleaned, verified list, no-website leads flagged as priority)
- Build the pipeline dashboard
- Build the Opener agent; generate the full script pack (first-touch ×3, reply pitch ×2 variants, objections, opt-out)
- ✅ Milestone: everything ready to start outreach

**Phase 2 — Outreach & lead magnets**
- 50 touches/day: texts first, calls to non-responders, SiteSmith builds free sites for every interested reply
- ✅ Milestone: first sales call booked

**Phase 3 — First paid engagement end-to-end**
- Close, invoice via Stripe, Fulfiller delivers (site live + AI intake + GBP cleanup), test deliverables, hand off, pitch the $100/mo upsell
- ✅ Milestone: **$500 invoice paid — scoreboard stops**

**Phase 4 — Systematize & scale**
- Package the agent fleet as reusable templates
- Re-run the playbook: next niche, next batch of leads, raise prices, stack recurring revenue
- ✅ Milestone: repeatable system producing clients on a schedule

## 9. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Low text reply rates | Scripts are personalization-first, no-pitch, local-sounding; A/B the three variants; calls + in-person as second channel |
| Niche exhausted without traction | Three pre-vetted backup niches; same workflow, zero retooling |
| Texting compliance (TCPA etc.) | Personal, one-to-one, conversational texts; instant honored opt-outs; mobile-verified numbers only; no automated blast sending — a human sends every first touch |
| "Free" offer sounds scammy | It genuinely is free (preview site with no strings); scripted honest objection handling; offer in-person meetings |
| Agent run costs | Reasoning effort tuned per task; scrape once per niche, not per lead |
| Fulfillment quality at scale | Fulfiller agent checklist + operator spot-checks before handoff; keep the bundle deliberately simple |
| Dependence on one operator's hour/day | The hour is only outreach; every other function is already agentized, so capacity scales by adding outreach hours or hiring a setter |

---

## 10. Guiding Principles

1. **Outreach is the only work that makes money.** Everything else is delegated or deleted.
2. **Give value before you pitch.** The free site does the selling.
3. **Price to remove the objection.** Raise prices only after proof.
4. **Sound like a neighbor, not a company.** Short texts, real name, "y'all."
5. **The scoreboard is cash collected.** Not conversations, not verbal yeses.
6. **60 minutes a day, every day, beats 8 hours once a week.**
