### 2.2 Property taxes — Weber County

- **Effective residential property-tax rate: ~0.60%** of market value (roughly 35% below the national average).
- Utah gives primary residences a **45% exemption** — only **55% of market value is taxable** — which is already baked into that ~0.60% effective figure.
- **Estimated annual tax on a $340–380k primary residence: ~$2,050–$2,300/yr (~$170–$190/mo).**

*Source: [Weber County Assessor — Tax Rates](https://www.webercountyutah.gov/Assessor/tax_rates.php); effective-rate figure via property-tax aggregators. **Confidence: Verified** on the exemption mechanism and approximate effective rate; the exact per-parcel rate varies by tax district (city + school + special districts) and must be confirmed on the specific parcel's tax notice.*

### 2.3 Flood and natural-hazard context

- Ogden sits at the base of the Wasatch Range and faces flooding from the **Ogden River, Weber River, and canyon drainages**, plus **flash flooding** and **Zone AO sheet-flow** areas.
- Common FEMA designations locally: **Zone AE, Zone AO, Zone X.** Homes in **AE/AO generally require flood insurance** with a federally-backed mortgage; **Zone X does not.**
- Broader Wasatch Front hazards to check per-parcel: **liquefaction/earthquake (Wasatch Fault), landslide on the benches, and radon** (common in Utah).

**Action:** For any candidate, run the exact address through the [FEMA Flood Map Service Center](https://msc.fema.gov/portal/search) and the [Weber County flood maps](https://www.webercountyutah.gov/Engineering/flood_maps.php). A property in Zone AE can add **$1,000–$3,000+/yr** in flood insurance — enough to erase an apparent price advantage.

*Source: [Weber County Engineering — Flood Maps](https://www.webercountyutah.gov/Engineering/flood_maps.php); [Utah Flood Hazards](https://floodhazards.utah.gov/). **Confidence: Verified** (general hazard context) / **Unknown** (any specific parcel until mapped).*

## 6. Basement & expansion analysis (framework)

Many in-budget Ogden homes are older ramblers with basements — often the real source of hidden value. For each candidate, evaluate and record:

- Ceiling height (≥7'; egress-bedroom code needs adequate window), natural light, existing egress window/well, exterior entrance, plumbing rough-ins, moisture indicators, foundation condition, HVAC/electrical access, stair location, layout efficiency.

**Rough improvement scenario template (fill per home):**
> Unfinished basement: ___ sqft · Realistically finishable: ___ sqft · Possible additions: bedroom / bath / family room / storage · Cost band: Low / Moderate / High · Primary risk: ceiling height / moisture / egress / plumbing / permits / layout.

**Hard rule:** never call a basement room a *legal bedroom* without a verified **egress window** and permit history. "Non-conforming bedroom" is a value and safety issue, and it is a top cause of misleading bedroom counts.

## 7. Lot, garage & parking framework

**Lot:** total sqft/acreage, backyard usability, slope/drainage, fence condition, vehicle access, RV parking, ADU potential (verify zoning), easements/utility placement. *A large lot only counts when it's actually usable — steep bench lots often aren't.*

## 9. Condition categories (sort every candidate)

- **A — Move-in ready:** paint/flooring/kitchen/baths functional, no imminent major systems.
- **B — Cosmetic opportunity:** paint, fixtures, cabinets, flooring, landscaping. *Often the best value — scares off other buyers, fixable cheaply.*
- **C — Moderate renovation:** kitchen/bath remodel, windows, HVAC, roof, partial basement finish. *Confirm the price leaves room for the work.*
- **D — Major risk:** foundation, structural movement, major water/sewer, mold, fire, extensive rewiring, unpermitted additions. *Not a top family pick unless you specifically want a project.*

**In a below-median budget, Category B in a decent Ogden micro-location is the sweet spot for "spectacular for the money."**

## 10. Negotiation & risk playbook

**Lawful seller-flexibility signals to look for (public only):** high DOM, multiple price cuts, back-on-market after a failed contract, vacant home, "motivated seller / bring offers / seller will contribute," weak photos, off-season timing, price sitting just above a round threshold ($385k → negotiable under $380k), builder incentives, assumable financing, offered rate buydown. *Do not infer private hardship without public disclosure.*

**Risk labels to assign per home** (Verified / Likely / Possible / Unknown / No indication):
flood zone · basement water history · radon · landslide/slope · earthquake/liquefaction · sewer line · galvanized/polybutylene plumbing · knob-and-tube/aluminum wiring · **Federal Pacific or Zinsco panel** (safety hazard, common in older Ogden homes) · unpermitted additions/bedrooms · shared driveway · easements/encroachments · retaining walls · busy road / rail / airport noise · steep driveway · solar lease/leased equipment · special assessments · square-footage discrepancy vs. county record.

**Never present a possible risk as a confirmed defect until verified.**
